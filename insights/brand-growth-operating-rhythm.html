The 90-day brand growth rhythm: discover, plan, execute, improve.

Good growth work is not a collection of campaigns. It is a rhythm the organisation can sustain.

When strategy, marketing, commerce and creative run on different calendars, every month starts with a re-argument about priorities. One shared rhythm removes that argument. Here is the four-step rhythm we use, and what each step must produce before the next one starts.

Step 1 — Discover (week 1)

Before proposing anything, map what actually exists: brand positioning and messaging; channels across search, paid, social, email and marketplace; the store funnel, conversion blockers and tracking; the data, including where numbers disagree; and the three bottlenecks that, if fixed, would unblock everything else.

Output: a one-page diagnostic a founder can read in five minutes. No 60-slide deck.

Step 2 — Plan (weeks 1–2)

Agree priorities, scope and success measures in one shared roadmap. What are we doing in the next 30 days? Who owns it? What number tells us it worked? What are we explicitly not doing this month?

The fourth question matters most. Growth teams fail by accepting every request. A short “not now” list is what makes the first three answerable.

Output: a roadmap with owners, deliverables and three agreed measures — one growth, one commerce, one efficiency.

Step 3 — Execute (weeks 2–10)

Work moves in focused sprints. Strategy, creative and technical execution sit under one roof, so a decision made on Monday can be live by Thursday without a vendor handoff.

A sprint is not “a busy two weeks”. It has a defined deliverable, a named owner and a review at the end. If it produces activity but no artefact, it was not a sprint.

Output: shipped work — a live page, a working funnel, a published campaign, a fixed feed, a documented automation.

Step 4 — Improve (weekly and at day 90)

Weekly reporting answers three questions in plain English: what changed, what it affected, and what happens next. At day 90, zoom out: which channel produced qualified demand, which produced noise, and what should be doubled or cut.

Output: a decision, not just a dashboard.

What makes the rhythm stick

  • One accountable owner for the whole picture, not just one function.
  • Scope approved before work starts — surprise work destroys the roadmap.
  • AI-assisted, human-reviewed — faster drafts, with a person responsible for anything customer-facing.
  • Measures agreed in the plan — if a measure was not agreed upfront, it cannot judge the work later.

FAQ

How is this different from hiring an agency?

Most agencies sell a channel — ads, SEO, social. This model sells the operating system that connects them, with one roadmap and one accountable team.

Can we start with just one function?

Yes. Start with the pair that has the most friction between them — usually growth and commerce, or brand and creative — and add the rest once the rhythm holds.

What if we already have good vendors?

Keep them. The rhythm still works: one roadmap, one reporting format, one person accountable for the handoffs.
